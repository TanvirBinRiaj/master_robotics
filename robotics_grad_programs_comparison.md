# Top University Robotics Graduate Programs — Structured Comparison

Research date: 2026-10-08. Sources: official program pages, course catalogs, syllabi, and publisher pages (cited inline).

---

## 1. Program Structures & Required Core Coursework

### MIT (no standalone Robotics dept — robotics lives in Course 2 MechE, Course 6 EECS, Course 16 AeroAstro, and CSAIL Robotics Center)

**Graduate course menu (CSAIL Robotics Center):**
- 2.152 Nonlinear Control Systems Design; **2.165 Robotics**; **2.166 Probabilistic Techniques for Mobile Robotics**; 2.183 Biomechanics/Neural Control of Movement
- 6.231 Dynamic Programming and Stochastic Control; 6.241 Dynamic Systems and Control; 6.245 Multivariate Control Systems; 6.435 Theory of Learning and System Identification; 6.437 Inference and Information; 6.438 Algorithms for Estimation and Inference; 6.801/6.866 Machine Vision; **6.832 Underactuated Robotics**; 6.867 Machine Learning
- **16.412 Cognitive Robotics** (B. Williams); **16.420 Planning Under Uncertainty**; 16.422 Human Supervisory Control
- Undergrad feeder sequence: 6.141/16.415J & 6.142/16.416J Robotics Systems and Science I/II; 2.12 Introduction to Robotics; 16.410 Principles of Autonomy

**Degree mechanics:** MIT has no "MS in Robotics"; students enter MechE/EECS/AeroAstro PhD or SM programs. EECS: SM thesis is a milestone of the PhD; PhD requires SM thesis + doctoral thesis; Graduate Division requires min. 2 years (4 semesters) residence and an oral qualifying exam on the research field. Requirements are department-set, not institute-wide.

### Stanford (robotics via CS, ME, Aero/Astro — no standalone MS in Robotics)

- **CS223A / ME320 Introduction to Robotics** (Khatib): geometry, kinematics, statics, dynamics, motion planning, control; taught with physical robot platforms + simulations. Text: course reader; supplementary *Robotics: Modelling, Planning and Control* (Siciliano et al.); reference: Craig, Spong.
- **AA 174A/274A/CS 237A/EE 260A** (Stanford ASL): planning/control of aerospace-robotics systems.
- Follow-ons: CS225A (experimental robotics), CS327A/CS326 (research robotics).
- Prereq: matrix algebra recommended (CS223A).

### CMU Robotics Institute

**MS in Robotics (MSR) — research master's (2 years, mimics PhD years 1–2):**
- 168 CMU credits: ≥84 coursework + ≥84 supervised research
- 4 core courses + 3 electives; four foundational areas: **Sensing & Perception, Thinking about Actions (cognition), Robot Embodiment, Environment Interaction**; depth in two areas
- Culminates in thesis document + public thesis talk; elective pool = all RI 16-600+ courses

**MS in Computer Vision (MSCV):** 111 units; core: 16-820 Advanced Computer Vision, 16-621/16-622 MSCV Capstone, summer internship; options: 10-601 Intro ML / 16-831 Intro Robot Learning, 15-663 / 16-824, 16-822 Geometry-based CV / 16-833 Robot Localization & Mapping

**Key math course — 16-811 Math Fundamentals for Robotics (Erdmann):** linear systems, polynomial interpolation, nonlinear equation solving, orthogonal-function approximation (Fourier), ODE integration, **optimization**, **calculus of variations** (Lagrangian dynamics, Principle of Least Action), **probability & stochastic processes (Markov chains)**, computational geometry (convex hull, Voronoi), **differential geometry**, **algebraic topology**

**16-311 Introduction to Robotics:** vision, motion planning, mobile mechanisms, kinematics, inverse kinematics, sensors; team-based LEGO Mindstorms labs (EE + ME + CS teams of 3). References: Corke; Craig; McKerrow; **Lynch & Park, Modern Robotics**

### UC Berkeley (robotics via EECS — CS/EE PhD or CS MS; BAIR/ Berkeley Robot Learning Lab)

- **EE C106A / EECS 106A Introduction to Robotics** (undergrad gateway): forward/inverse kinematics, Jacobian, force relations, dynamics, control. Text: **Murray, Li & Sastry, A Mathematical Introduction to Robotic Manipulation** (Berkeley's own book — Lie groups, screw calculus, nonholonomic planning, Frobenius theorem in appendix)
- **CS 287 Advanced Robotics** (Abbeel): "master the math and algorithms underneath state-of-the-art robotic systems… heavily based on probabilistic reasoning and optimization." Topics: MDPs, value/policy iteration, LP, discretization, function approximation, LQR/iLQR/DDP, nonlinear & convex optimization (Boyd & Vandenberghe ch. 9–11), EKF/UKF, smoothers, MAP/EM, POMDPs, EKF-SLAM/Graph-SLAM. Grading: assignments 60% + open-ended project 40%. Goal: read ICRA/IROS/RSS/IJRR/T-RO papers.

### ETH Zurich — MSc Robotics, Systems and Control (D-MAVT + D-ITET + D-INFK)

90 ECTS, 1.5 years, English. Qualifying BSc: EE, MechE, CS.
- **Core Courses 36 CP** (tutor-approved individual program)
- Multidisciplinary Courses 6 CP (D-MAVT/D-INFK/D-ITET/Uni Zürich/HSG, master level)
- Science in Perspective 2 CP (D-GESS humanities/social science)
- **Semester Project 8 CP** (supervised by ETH professor)
- **Industrial Internship 8 CP** (≥12 weeks, Swiss or foreign company/research institute)
- **Master's Thesis 30 CP** (tied to tutor's research)
- Required seminar: ETH Distinguished Seminar in Robotics, Systems and Controls (100% attendance)
- Content areas: robot design, modelling & control, systems engineering, physical modelling & simulation, optimisation & control, perception/graphics/VR, navigation & path planning, embedded & distributed computing, AI

### Georgia Tech (IRIM — MS and PhD in Robotics, home school = AE/BME/ECE/IC/ME/Physics)

**MS (36 semester hours, letter grade, GPA ≥3.0; ≤6 hrs of 4000-level):**
- Fundamentals 12 h: 7785 Introduction to Robotics Research; 7741–7743 Robotics Professional Preparation (1 h each); 8740 Robotics Internship (0 h, summer); **8741 Robotics Capstone Project (6 h)**
- Core Areas 18 h: choose 3 of {Mechanics, Controls, Perception, AI, HRI (+Natural Systems)}, each with 1 foundation + 1 elective (e.g., AE 6210 Advanced Dynamics I; AE 6530 Multivariable Linear Systems & Control; ME 6407 Robotics; CS 6601 AI; CS 6476 Computer Vision; CS 7633 HRI)
- Open electives 6 h
- Thesis option: 9 thesis hours, 3 open elective hours

**PhD (2025–26 Handbook):**
- 33 semester hours letter-grade
- **Qualifying exam, two components:** (1) course-based GPA ≥3.5 in 4 courses from exactly 2 distinct core areas, 2 of them foundation courses, completed by end of 6th semester; (2) comprehensive oral exam by ≥3 robotics faculty (incl. advisor), first attempt before end of 5th semester, 1 retake allowed by 8th semester
- Teaching apprenticeship; minor field (2 courses, 6 h)
- Proposal → "Request for Admission to PhD Candidacy" → dissertation defense (≥6 months between proposal approval and defense scheduling)

### University of Michigan (standalone Robotics dept — MS & PhD)

**MS (30 credits letter-graded):**
- Core 8 cr: **ROB 501 Math for Robotics** (4 cr, Fall, required semester 1) + **ROB 550 Robotics Systems Lab** (4 cr, required within first 2 semesters)
- Breadth ≥9 cr (≥1 course each in Sensing, Reasoning, Acting); Depth ≥3 cr; Directed Study ROB 590 (3–6 cr); optional ROB 502 Programming for Robotics, ROB 690 Advanced Research
- Grade: B− or better; cumulative Rackham GPA ≥3.0

**PhD (36 credits; 26 cr for entering MS holders; ≥18 of 36 at Michigan):**
- Same core + breadth/depth + 6 cr beyond the MS; grade B+ or higher in all counted courses; cumulative GPA ≥3.5
- Dissertation proposal exam & defense (≥1 year before final defense)

**ROB 501 syllabus (public):** abstract linear algebra, subspaces, eigenvalues, norms, inner-product spaces, projection theorem, Gram-Schmidt, normal equations/least squares, PSD matrices, Schur complement, recursive least squares, **Kalman filter**, BLUE, probability spaces, random variables, Gaussian random vectors, real analysis (normed spaces, compactness, Cauchy sequences, Weierstrass), **Newton-Raphson**, convexity, **linear & quadratic programming**

Breadth examples — Sensing: EECS 504/542 CV, EECS 564 Estimation/Filtering/Detection, ROB 599 Deep Learning for Robot Perception; Reasoning: ROB 520 Motion Planning, ROB 511 ROS, EECS 545/553 ML, IOE 511 Continuous Optimization, EECS 558 Stochastic Control, MATH 525 Probability Theory; Acting: ROB 510 Kinematics & Dynamics, EECS 560 Linear Systems Theory, EECS 562 Nonlinear Systems & Control, ROB 530 Mobile Robotics

### University of Utah Robotics Center (reference point — clean 4-core structure)

MS core 12 cr: ROBOT 6000 Robotics I: Mechanics; ROBOT 6100 Robotics II: Control; ROBOT 6200 Motion Planning *or* CS 6300 AI; CS 6640 Image Processing *or* CS 6320 Computer Vision. PhD: 30 course credits + 3 seminar + 14 dissertation; written qualifier + proposal defense (oral) both ≥1 semester before dissertation defense; proposal follows NSF format (budget, biosketch, 15-page project description).

## 3. Mathematical Prerequisites (synthesized across programs)

**Universal entry requirements (stated by programs/books):** linear algebra (matrix operations, rank, eigen-decomposition), multivariate calculus, ODEs, probability & statistics, programming (Python/MATLAB/C++).

**Graduate-level math taught as *courses*, not assumed:**
- **Optimization:** convex/nonlinear optimization, LP/QP, dynamic programming, calculus of variations (Michigan ROB 501; CMU 16-811; Berkeley CS287; Washington ME 578/AA 578; Michigan IOE 511/611)
- **Stochastic processes:** Markov chains, Kalman filtering, estimation theory, stochastic control (Michigan ROB 501 + EECS 558; CMU 16-811; MIT 6.231; Berkeley CS287 EKF/UKF/SLAM)
- **Differential geometry & Lie theory:** SO(3)/SE(3), Lie algebra se(3), exponential map, twists/screws, adjoint representation, Frobenius theorem, manifolds (Northwestern ME 450 "Geometry in Robotics" — full syllabus: manifolds, tangent/cotangent/fiber bundles, Lie groups, Lie derivatives, distributions, Riemannian metrics, Christoffel symbols; CMU 16-811 differential geometry + algebraic topology; Berkeley EE C106A/Murray-Li-Sastry appendix; modern robotics PhD theses use Lie-group state estimation, e.g., Michigan "Contact-Aided State Estimation on Lie Groups for Legged Robots")
- **Real analysis basics:** normed spaces, compactness, convergence, contraction mappings (Michigan ROB 501)
- **Computational geometry:** convex hulls, Voronoi diagrams (CMU 16-811)

**Note:** Lie theory/differential geometry is typically *learned inside* robotics courses (Modern Robotics Ch. 2–5; ME 450) rather than required beforehand — but PhD-level geometric control, estimation on manifolds, and nonholonomic planning assume it.

## 5. What a PhD Graduate Knows That a Book Reader Doesn't

Evidence from program handbooks (Georgia Tech qualifying-exam goals; Utah proposal requirements; Berkeley CS287 course goals; Michigan/CMU research requirements):

1. **Original problem formulation.** Books teach solved problems; PhDs learn to *find* the problem — identify gaps in literature, formalize an unsolved task, and defend it (GT quals: "conduct independent research and review, synthesize, and evaluate previous work").
2. **Qualifying-exam stamina.** Oral defense of broad + deep knowledge before a 3+ faculty committee (GT), written + oral qualifiers (Utah), dissertation proposal exam (Michigan), POMDP-literature-based exams (MIT 16.412 readings include PhD theses).
3. **Research-grade implementation.** Debugging real hardware: sensor noise, calibration drift, latency, real-time constraints, ROS integration (Michigan ROB 550/511; CMU 16-311 labs) — failure modes absent from textbook exercises.
4. **Paper reproduction & critique.** Reading ICRA/IROS/RSS/IJRR/T-RO papers, reproducing results, designing ablations and statistically sound experiments (explicit CS287 goal).
5. **Proposal & grant writing.** NSF-format proposals with budget, biosketch, 15-page project description, timeline (Utah PhD proposal defense requirement).
6. **Teaching & communication.** Teaching apprenticeship (GT PhD), public thesis talks (CMU MSR), seminar participation (ETH Distinguished Seminar).
7. **Mathematical fluency beyond recipes.** Deriving (not just applying) estimators/controllers — e.g., working on SE(3) with Lie Jacobians, deriving Lagrangian dynamics via calculus of variations, proving convergence/compactness arguments (CMU 16-811; Michigan ROB 501 "from computation to proof").
8. **Research lifecycle ownership.** Ethics/IRB considerations, reproducibility, open-sourcing, peer review, and knowing when a result is publishable — the tacit curriculum between milestone exams.

**Bottom line:** A book reader (even all five core texts) acquires the *codified knowledge* — kinematics, dynamics, planning, estimation. A PhD adds the *uncodified capabilities*: problem discovery, committee-tested depth, hardware-grounded debugging, literature synthesis, and funded original contribution.
